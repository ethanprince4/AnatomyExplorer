# Anatomy Explorer v4.0.4

**Installing on a Mac.** The installer is now a disk image, `Install-Anatomy-Explorer-Mac.dmg`. Open it and
double-click **Install Anatomy Explorer** in the window that appears; there is no zip to unpack and nothing to look
for in Downloads or Applications.

**Large 3D models draw far less.** Several models reach from a whole organ down to single cells, and at their opening
view most of their triangles were smaller than a pixel. Those parts now carry coarser copies, and each frame draws a
part at the coarsest copy whose detail is still under a pixel where it sits, so the picture does not change. At the
opening view the kidney draws a third as many triangles, the neuron a quarter, thin skin a sixth and the pancreas half;
zoomed in, the parts in view keep their full mesh. Models with no such detail are unchanged. The copies are made while
a model loads, which adds a few seconds on the largest models.

**Histology labels.** 603 of the 991 slides now have numbered labels on the image; the others already print their own
labels, are diagrams, or show nothing that can be named with confidence. Labels look like an atlas plate: a ring on the
structure, a leader line and a dark tag with the number and name, placed so tags do not cover each other or the points
they name.

**Radiology.** A scan's numbered labels can now sit at their own places on one 3D structure: a hip film's femoral head,
neck, trochanters and shaft each get their number on the femur, where before only one of them could. Each case's labels
were placed from the scan and the 3D view, then checked by a second pass that moved or removed any on the wrong
landmark. Cases whose 3D view hid labelled structures, cut them off or drew a finding that could not be seen were
corrected. Together, 537 of the 627 scan labels now appear on the 3D reference (468 in 4.0.3); a label with nothing
to point at in 3D stays on the scan.
