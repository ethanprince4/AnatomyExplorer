1. The panels

Today the selection label is a light paper card, and the dock is a box. Both are the brightest things on screen after the body. Three ways to keep them on the wall instead are below, each applied to every screen and to the website.

Today, on other screens

Wall recommended

The label is lettered straight onto the wall, in the same type as the Radiology column, with hairline rules and outlined buttons. The dock is the tools lettered in a row under one hairline, with the brass underline on the active tool.

Our pick. The label reads as part of the wall, like lettering in a museum, so the body stays the one bright thing on screen, and it has the most contrast headroom of the three.

Plaque

The same lettering on a panel of the wall’s own colour, a little lighter, with a hairline highlight on the top edge and small corners. The dock is one low strip of the same material.

Frame

The edge of a vitrine: a fine hairline with corner marks, and the wall showing through. The dock is an outlined strip with one cell per tool.

Notes

  • On the website the two “Download” buttons stay filled brass, to match the one at the top. The brief said brass outline. Should they be outlined too?
  • All three pass contrast in all six schemes. To get there, the button outlines are a little brighter than in Radiology.
  • The frame’s long hairline is faint on purpose; its corner marks carry the shape.
  • The pictures in the website’s Study section still show the old paper cards. They will be rendered again once you pick.

2. The logo

Three options, side by side at the same sizes, from the app icon down to a 16 px browser tab. At 32 px all three still read as a person. At 16 px Section and Quarter hold up best; Peel becomes a figure beside two blocks.

1Peel

Leonardo’s figure on three sheets of glass, one behind the other: the skin in front with the circle, the muscle in brass, then the skeleton. It is the most direct answer to “a human, clearly layered, in 3D”, but it is a picture more than a sign, and small sizes need simpler drawings.

Built to Leonardo’s proportions; the 64, 32 and 16 px drawings were each checked pixel by pixel, and the 32 px drawing keeps a heavy circle. See the explorations.

2Section

Leonardo’s figure in its circle, with a quarter of the skin cut away from the navel up, so the muscle shows one step deeper. It is the most logo-like of the three and keeps its circle down to 32 px.

Same figure and checks as Peel; the arms have the same tapered muscle as the legs. At 16 px the brass shoulder can look like a raised flag. See the explorations.

3Quarter

The app’s own body model, standing, with the skin cut away from its upper right quarter so the real muscle layer shows in brass. The calmest of the three, and the closest to the app itself.

Traced from the app’s skin and muscle renders; the four small drawings are fitted to the pixel grid and checked at 1× and 4×. It is narrow in a square tile, and below 64 px it reads as a figure in two colours. See the explorations.

Also finished: Vitrine, the body as three glass plates in a museum case. It is beautiful large, but at small sizes it reads as two or three people, and it repeats Peel’s idea. In all, nearly sixty drawings were tried across the four (Vitrine’s explorations).
