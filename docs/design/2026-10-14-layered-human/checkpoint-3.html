B. Search

The app’s search holds 4,717 entries: 2,422 structures, 563 groups, 766 landmarks, 418 clinical notes, 303 lessons, 104 radiology cases, 100 tissues and 41 models. It matches the whole name first, then the start of the name, then the start of each word, then anywhere, with Latin names counted a little lower: “bic” finds 72 and “biceps” 17. It forgives a typo only when nothing else matched, so “bycep” finds nothing today, and its list says nothing about where a structure is on the body.

All three options use the app’s own matcher and a slice of its real index. They fold a muscle’s parts under it, as a printed index does, show no key hint, and include a proposed one-line change so that “bycep” finds 19.

What is not finished

So that nothing here looks more finished than it is:

  • Labels: the Qt app paints its labels, so keyboard focus and screen-reader support for the circles would be new work, and each lesson needs a map from its bold terms to their structures.
  • Labels: Hunt mode is described, not drawn. In Umber the selected circle and the open circles differ only by hue.
  • Search: the mockup has only the biceps figure, points for 16 muscles and 802 of the app’s 4,717 entries, so choosing anything else only renames the label.
  • Search: option 1 needs an “is it in view?” check per result that the app does not have, and the “bycep” fix is a proposed change to the app’s matcher.
  • Navigation: the Practice room’s front page is described, not drawn; the 1280 px pictures scale the full-size room under the new bar; the Back targets and saved views are a made-up session.
  • Navigation: the slide’s link to its model needs a lookup from tissue to model that the app does not have yet.
  • Histology: the body map’s eight circles were placed by eye. Ten tissues have no micrograph and show an empty frame, and some micrographs show a pointer or an eyepiece circle.
  • Histology: “Look for” text exists for only two tissues; the data has no field for it or for the stain.
